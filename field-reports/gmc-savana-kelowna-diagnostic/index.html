A work crew from Vancouver texted us early one morning: their 2016 GMC Savana was leaking water and they didn't know why. They were just passing through Kelowna.

We answered right away. Every case we take starts with a text, and the same certified mechanic handles it from that first message through to the finished repair. The crew explained the problem once, to the person who would end up fixing it.

We scheduled an on-site diagnostic for that same morning. The quote comes after a certified mechanic has been to the vehicle with the right equipment and found the actual problem, which is why we don't put a number on a repair we haven't seen yet.

We only found out about the two-week quotes from other shops once we were already there, running the diagnostic. The crew hadn't mentioned it in the first text. It came up while we were working on the van.

How we confirmed it was the water pump

A coolant leak can come from more than one place: a hose, a clamp, a cracked reservoir, or the pump itself. Each one looks the same from underneath the van, just a puddle of coolant on the ground. Guess wrong and the crew pays for a part that was never the problem, then waits again while the real cause keeps leaking.

Before touching anything, we ran a pressure test on the cooling system right there in the driveway. The van never left the spot where the crew parked it. We hooked a pressure tester to the system and brought it up to the same pressure the engine hits when it's running hot.

The hoses and clamps held steady. The needle didn't move on those, so we crossed them off the list. Then the reading dropped, and it dropped only at the water pump, not slowly across the whole system. That kind of drop, sharp and isolated to one spot, means that one part is failing on its own, not the lines around it.

The pump's internal seal had let go. Fairly common on a van with this kind of mileage and use. We ruled out the fitting and the hose before we ever touched the pump, so by the time we named it as the cause, there wasn't much left to guess about.

We keep the gauge in view while this happens so the client sees the same reading we do. Nobody has to take our word for it.

What the test ruled in, and what it ruled out

The pressure test named one part: the water pump had failed internally, and that's where the coolant under the van was coming from. Nothing else on the system was leaking. The hoses and clamps held their seal under the same test, and so did the reservoir.

That mattered for the repair itself. A second leak somewhere else would have meant reopening the system after the pump was already replaced, on a van that was supposed to be back on the road that evening. Because the test isolated one cause, the whole repair fit into a single visit to the same driveway.

The pump we installed was a genuine GM part, not an aftermarket substitute, and it was available locally the same afternoon. That's not always the case on every vehicle. When it isn't, the timeline changes, and we tell the client that up front instead of promising a same-day fix we can't deliver.

Getting the crew back on the road the same day

With the cause confirmed, we sourced a genuine OEM water pump that same afternoon, locally, without a special order. The old pump came out, the new one went in.

Installing the part is only half the job. After a repair like this, the cooling system has air trapped in it that has to be bled out, or the engine can run hot even with a good pump. We refilled the system, bled the air out, and ran the engine to confirm the pressure held steady under load, not just at idle.

While the pump was already out, we found a second issue with the van and fixed that too, in the same visit. We showed the crew what we'd found and agreed the price by text before touching it, so it went onto the same bill they'd already seen. One trip covered both repairs, and the call-out was charged once.

The van was ready at about 7 PM, the same day the crew first texted us that morning. Other shops in town had quoted them two weeks just to look at it. The crew drove back toward Vancouver that night.

Questions drivers ask about this kind of repair

Is it safe to keep driving with a coolant leak?

No. A coolant leak means the engine can overheat, and an overheated engine turns a contained repair into a much larger one, up to head gasket or internal engine damage. If you see coolant under the vehicle or the temperature gauge starts climbing, stop driving it and have it checked where it sits. That is the situation this crew was in: they stopped, we came to the van, and the failure stayed limited to the pump.

How do you know it's the water pump and not just a hose?

We run a pressure test on the cooling system on-site. If the hoses and clamps hold pressure but the reading drops at the pump, that confirms the pump itself has failed internally. You see the gauge and the failed part before approving any repair.

Can you fix a coolant leak the same day if I'm from out of town?

Yes, when the part is available locally. This crew texted us early one morning and their 2016 GMC Savana was finished at about 7 PM the same day, after other shops had quoted them two weeks. We source the part ourselves rather than sending you to find one.

How much does a water pump repair cost in Kelowna?

It depends on the vehicle and how much has to come off to reach the pump. We don't publish flat prices online because every case is different. What we do is agree the itemized price with you before any work starts, and the final bill matches the figure you approved.

Do you work on commercial vans and fleet vehicles?

Yes. We regularly diagnose and repair work vans and fleet vehicles on-site, including for crews passing through Kelowna who can't afford to wait days for a shop appointment.

What if the water pump isn't the actual problem?

We test the full cooling system on-site with a pressure gauge before naming a cause, and you are standing there while we do it. Once the part is out, we hand it to you. By the time you approve a repair, you have seen the reading that found the fault and the component that caused it.
